# Risk Reversal

## The Big Idea

Buyers are not afraid of the price.
They are afraid of paying and getting nothing.

Every seller says "trust me". Almost none say "you pay only if it works".
The second one wins because it cannot be faked cheaply.

## The Moves

1. **Pay-on-result beats every guarantee.**
   A money-back promise is words. Paying AFTER the result is structure.
   "Nothing upfront. You see it done, then you pay."

2. **Say the quiet part loudly.**
   "You were going to judge us by results anyway. We just put it in writing."
   You are not giving a new gift, just naming the deal honestly.

3. **Free first step at your risk.**
   One free demonstration converts skeptics like nothing else:
   "First one is on me. If it works, you pay for that one plus the rest."

4. **Batch gates for big distrust.**
   "We do batches of five. Each batch paid only after you verify it."
   Trust grows in verified steps.

5. **Even a NO leaves them ahead.**
   Give something keepable: an audit, a checklist, real information.
   Worst case becomes "I still got value" instead of "I wasted my time".

6. **Never reverse risk you can't carry.**
   A promise you can't honor is failure mode number one. Size the offer to
   what operations can actually deliver.

## When It Backfires

- Reversing risk with fine print. Conditions kill the effect. Keep it plain
  and short enough to screenshot.
- Using it on buyers who would have paid anyway. Save it for hesitators;
  overusing trains people to stall for discounts.

## One-Line Memory

Whoever carries the risk controls the close. Take their risk, and the sale
closes itself.
